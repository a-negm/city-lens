# Product Purpose

## Product name
Working title: Berlin Urban Health Map

## Summary
This product helps people understand how urban conditions in Berlin may affect daily and long-term health. It combines environmental, health, and social context data into a map-first experience that supports better awareness and better decisions.

## Core problem
City conditions affect inhabitants in uneven ways. Air quality, green space, heat, social context, and other urban factors vary across Berlin, but this information is fragmented, hard to compare, and difficult to interpret.

## Product goal
Turn public city data into a clear, spatial product that helps users understand:
- what conditions exist in a given area
- how those conditions compare with other parts of Berlin
- what factors may matter most
- what users can reasonably do with that information

## Who this is for
Primary:
- residents of Berlin
- people exploring neighborhoods
- health-conscious urban users
- people interested in how city conditions affect daily life

Secondary:
- designers, researchers, and hiring managers reviewing this as a portfolio case study

## User needs
Users need to:
- inspect an area on a map
- understand the health-related context of that area
- compare areas across Berlin
- see which factors are strongest or weakest
- understand uncertainty and limitations

## MVP scope
The first version should:
- use a 2D map
- focus on a small set of strong signals
- allow area selection
- show a clear side panel with key metrics
- support one active layer at a time
- use deterministic scoring logic
- optionally add AI only for explanation, not for core logic

## Initial data priorities
Use strongest and clearest signals first:
- air quality
- green space
- income or social context
- life expectancy or health context where available

## Non-goals
This product is not:
- a medical diagnostic tool
- a clinical risk predictor
- a real-time emergency alert system
- a full scientific model of causation
- a complete representation of all city data
- a chatbot-first product

## Product thesis
Urban health is shaped by environmental exposure and social context. This product helps users understand those patterns spatially and interpret them responsibly.

## Success criteria for MVP
The MVP is successful if a user can:
- select an area
- understand the main factors affecting that area
- compare it with Berlin average
- compare it with another area
- leave with a clearer understanding, not just more raw data
